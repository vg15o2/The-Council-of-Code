### 1. User Input / Output
Before you can cook any algorithms, you need to communicate with the outside world. This means input/output in the form of data—getting values into your program and getting the results back out.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Use the fastest I/O methods available in your language of choice. This will save time when dealing with large data sets, making you safer in coding competitions or interviews.

---

### 2. Data Types
Everything in programming boils down to data. Understanding data types is like understanding the raw ingredients of a recipe—you need to know what you’re working with before you start cooking up a storm.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Don’t waste memory. Use the smallest data type that can hold your values. `int` is often the default, but `byte` and `short` can save space in certain scenarios.

---

### 3. Conditionals (If-Else, Switch)
The decision-making process is key—whether it's `if`, `else`, or a `switch` statement, your program needs to know what to do next.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Use `switch` when you have multiple options based on a single variable—faster than a long chain of `if-else` statements.

---

### 4. Loops
You can’t escape from loops—they’re like the repeating cycles in every drug cartel operation. `For`, `while`, and `do-while` loops will keep you in an endless cycle until you’re able to break free from the problem-solving trap.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Use a `while` loop when you don’t know the number of iterations—it’s like running a cartel operation without a fixed end.

---

### 5. Functions
Now that you’ve got the basics, you need functions—the organized method of breaking down the work. Functions help you modularize your logic and keep your code clean and efficient.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Use recursive functions for problems that naturally break down into smaller sub-problems—like a drug empire expanding in phases.

---

### 6. Time Complexity
When you're working in a cartel, time is money—so you better understand how fast or slow your operations run. That’s what time complexity is all about: figuring out how your solution will scale.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Don’t go for brute force. Instead, optimize your algorithms to run in the fastest possible time. The best cartels know how to scale their operations with the least resources.

---

### 7. Basic Math
Before you get into the dirty work, you need to brush up on your basic math skills—everything from arithmetic operations to understanding modulo and number systems. You’ll use this math for time complexity analysis, working with arrays, and solving recursion problems.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Always round down when dealing with integers in your math operations. You don’t want to overestimate your resources.

---

### 8. Recursion
Now we’re talking recursion—the kind of infinite loop that the cartel loves. Functions calling themselves—just like the inner workings of an empire. Recursion will keep you coming back for more—solving problems with fewer lines of code, but more mental effort.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Remember, tail recursion can sometimes be optimized to iterative solutions—less memory used, more power.

---

### 9. Basic Hashing
In the world of the cartel, hashing is like a secret code. You need to store and look up data efficiently, and that’s where hash tables come in.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Use a hash set or hash map to handle large data sets efficiently. Hashing helps you check membership and find duplicates without wasting time.

---

### 10. Sorting Techniques
The cartel knows how to organize things—whether it's sorting money, drugs, or data. Sorting algorithms are crucial for processing large sets of data efficiently.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Use quick sort or merge sort when you need the best performance. But be wary—bubble sort might be too slow for the big leagues.

---

### 11. STL / Java Collections
Now we’re talking about the tools you use to build your empire. STL in C++ or Collections in Java are like the blueprints that help you construct your algorithmic cartel.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:  
Use vectors or lists in C++/Java to store data. They're fast and flexible when you need to organize data on the fly.

---

### 12. Logical Thinking
Finally, you need logical thinking—the ability to break down problems, identify patterns, and develop solutions. Every cartel operation is well-planned, and every code should be thought out before execution.

🔥 **Saul’s Loophole**:
